\section{Model sketch: multi-sector New Keynesian economy with an endogenous production network}\label{model-sketch-an-endogenous-production-network-inside-a-multi-sector-new-keynesian-economy}

This document builds the model from primitives. Every assumption is
listed once (Section 1) with a label, every actor's problem is written
and solved (Section 2), the flex-price equilibrium is derived exactly
(Section 3), the network-choice block is derived from the firm's problem
including a re-derivation of KMNT's Lemma 2 for monopolistic producers
(Section 4), the sticky-price block is log-linearized step by step and
mapped onto R23's objects (Section 5), inconsistencies and undesired
effects are listed and mapped to the assumptions that cause them
(Section 6), and a notation dictionary closes (Section 7). All
derivations are in the main text.

Conventions: lower-case letters are logs of the corresponding upper-case
variables (\(p_i = \log P_i\)); vectors are column vectors indexed over
sectors \(i = 0,1,\dots,N\); \(\mathbf{1}\) is the vector of ones;
\(e_0\) is the first unit vector; \(\mathbb{E}_t\) is the expectation
conditional on period-\(t\) information; a superscript \(nat\) denotes
the flexible-price (``natural'') value; a tilde denotes a gap from
natural. Sector \(0\) is the labor union.

\subsection{0. Overview and timing}\label{overview-and-timing}

There are three nested clocks.

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Regime.} A regime is a belief pair \((\mu,\Sigma)\) describing
  the stationary distribution of sectoral productivity. A fragmentation
  shock is a once-and-for-all change of regime.
\item
  \textbf{Network.} At the start of a regime, each sector chooses its
  input technique \(\Omega_i\) once, before any productivity is
  realized, on the basis of \((\mu,\Sigma)\). The technique stays fixed
  for the life of the regime.
\item
  \textbf{Period.} Every period \(t\), productivity \(\varepsilon_t\) is
  realized, a fraction of producers reset prices (Calvo), the central
  bank sets the nominal rate, and markets clear.
\end{enumerate}

The two blocks of the model correspond to clocks 2 and 3. Because the
technique is evaluated under the flexible-price distribution (assumption
I2 below), the network block is solved first and its output --- the
input-output matrix \(\Omega^*\), the Domar weights \(\lambda^*\), the
steady-state TFP levels \(a_i(\Omega_i^*)\) --- becomes the
linearization point of the sticky-price block. The structure is
recursive: no fixed point across blocks.

\subsection{2. Actors and their
problems}\label{actors-and-their-problems}

\subsubsection{2.1 Household}\label{household}

The household chooses consumption \(\{C_{it}\}\), raw hours \(L_t\) and
one-period nominal bonds \(B_t\) to maximize
\[\mathbb{E}_0\sum_{t=0}^\infty\rho^t\left[\frac{C_t^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}-\frac{L_t^{1+\phi}}{1+\phi}\right]\]
subject to
\[\sum_{i=1}^N P_{it}C_{it}+B_t\le W^h_tL_t+(1+i_{t-1})B_{t-1}+\Pi_t-T_t,\qquad C_t=\prod_{i=1}^N\left(\frac{C_{it}}{\beta_i}\right)^{\beta_i},\]
where \(W^h_t\) is the price at which the household sells raw hours to
the union, \(\Pi_t\) is aggregate profit (all sectors, including the
union) and \(T_t\) the lump-sum tax.

\textbf{Expenditure minimization.} Minimizing \(\sum_iP_{it}C_{it}\)
subject to \(\prod_i(C_{it}/\beta_i)^{\beta_i}=1\) with multiplier
\(\Lambda^C\) gives \(P_{it}=\Lambda^C\beta_i/C_{it}\), hence
\(C_{it}=\Lambda^C\beta_i/P_{it}\). Substituting into the constraint,
\(\prod_i(\Lambda^C/P_{it})^{\beta_i}=1\), so
\(\Lambda^C=\prod_iP_{it}^{\beta_i}\equiv P^C_t\). Therefore
\[C_{it}=\beta_i\frac{P^C_tC_t}{P_{it}},\qquad P^C_t=\prod_{i=1}^NP_{it}^{\beta_i},\qquad p^C_t=\beta^\top p_t. \tag{2.1}\]
The CPI is an exact geometric average; no log-linearization is needed.

\textbf{Labor supply.} The first-order condition for \(L_t\) with the
multiplier on the budget constraint \(\Lambda_t\rho^t\) gives
\(L_t^\phi=\Lambda_tW^h_t\), and the condition for \(C_t\) gives
\(C_t^{-\gamma}=\Lambda_tP^C_t\). Combining,
\[\frac{W^h_t}{P^C_t}=C_t^\gamma L_t^\phi\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad w^h_t=p^C_t+\gamma c_t+\phi l_t. \tag{2.2}\]
Under P4 there is a single marginal rate of substitution for all hours,
whatever labor type they are supplied as.

\textbf{Euler equation.} The condition for \(B_t\),
\(\rho^t\Lambda_t=\rho^{t+1}(1+i_t)\mathbb{E}_t\Lambda_{t+1}\), with
\(\Lambda_t=C_t^{-\gamma}/P^C_t\), is
\[1=\rho(1+i_t)\,\mathbb{E}_t\left[\left(\frac{C_{t+1}}{C_t}\right)^{-\gamma}\frac{P^C_t}{P^C_{t+1}}\right]. \tag{2.3}\]

\textbf{Stochastic discount factor.} The nominal state price for a
period-\(t+s\) payoff, in units of period-\(t\) money, is
\[\Lambda_{t,t+s}=\rho^s\left(\frac{C_{t+s}}{C_t}\right)^{-\gamma}\frac{P^C_t}{P^C_{t+s}}. \tag{2.4}\]
By M3 this is what every producer uses.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{2.2 Producers in sectors
\(i\ge1\)}{2.2 Producers in sectors i\textbackslash ge1}}\label{producers-in-sectors-ige1}

Producer \(f\) in sector \(i\) solves three problems: an ex-post input
choice every period, a Calvo price-reset problem whenever selected, and
an ex-ante technique choice once per regime.

\textbf{(a) Ex-post input choice.} Given technique \(\Omega_i\),
realized TFP and input prices, minimize \(\sum_{j=0}^NP_{jt}X_{ifjt}\)
subject to producing one unit. With multiplier \(\Lambda^X\), the
first-order condition for input \(j\) is
\(P_{jt}=\Lambda^X\Omega_{ij}/X_{ifjt}\) (using the constraint \(Y=1\)),
i.e.~\(X_{ifjt}=\Lambda^X\Omega_{ij}/P_{jt}\). Substituting into the
production constraint,
\[e^{\varepsilon_{it}+a_i}\zeta_i\prod_j\left(\frac{\Lambda^X\Omega_{ij}}{P_{jt}}\right)^{\Omega_{ij}}=1\;\Longrightarrow\;\Lambda^X=\frac{\prod_jP_{jt}^{\Omega_{ij}}}{e^{\varepsilon_{it}+a_i}\zeta_i\prod_j\Omega_{ij}^{\Omega_{ij}}}=\frac{\prod_jP_{jt}^{\Omega_{ij}}}{e^{\varepsilon_{it}+a_i(\Omega_i)}},\]
where the last step uses \(\zeta_i\prod_j\Omega_{ij}^{\Omega_{ij}}=1\).
The multiplier is the pre-subsidy unit cost, common to all producers in
the sector:
\[MC_{it}=\frac{\prod_{j=0}^NP_{jt}^{\Omega_{ij}}}{e^{\varepsilon_{it}+a_i(\Omega_i)}},\qquad mc_{it}=\sum_{j=0}^N\Omega_{ij}p_{jt}-\varepsilon_{it}-a_i(\Omega_i), \tag{2.5}\]
and conditional factor demands are
\(X_{ifjt}=\Omega_{ij}MC_{it}Y_{ift}/P_{jt}\). Cost shares are constant
and equal to \(\Omega_{ij}\) (T1, T4): this is the Cobb--Douglas
property that makes (2.5) exactly log-linear in prices and productivity.
With the subsidy (M2) the producer's effective marginal cost is
\((1-\tau)MC_{it}=MC_{it}/\mathcal{M}\).

\textbf{(b) Price setting.} Demand for variety \(f\) is
\(Y_{ift}=(P_{ift}/P_{it})^{-\theta}Y_{it}\) with sectoral index
\(P_{it}=\left(\int_0^1P_{ift}^{1-\theta}df\right)^{1/(1-\theta)}\). A
producer selected to reset in \(t\) (probability \(\delta_i\)) chooses
\(P^*_{it}\) to maximize the discounted value of profits over the
horizon during which the price survives:
\[\max_{P^*}\ \mathbb{E}_t\sum_{s=0}^\infty(1-\delta_i)^s\Lambda_{t,t+s}\left[P^*-\frac{MC_{i,t+s}}{\mathcal{M}}\right]\left(\frac{P^*}{P_{i,t+s}}\right)^{-\theta}Y_{i,t+s}.\]
Differentiating with respect to \(P^*\) and dividing by
\((1-\theta)(P^*)^{-\theta}\) gives:
\[P^*_{it} \;=\; \frac{\mathbb{E}_t \displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{\infty} (1-\delta_i)^s \, \Lambda_{t,t+s} \, P_{i,t+s}^{\theta} \, Y_{i,t+s} \, MC_{i,t+s}}
{\mathbb{E}_t \displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{\infty} (1-\delta_i)^s \, \Lambda_{t,t+s} \, P_{i,t+s}^{\theta} \, Y_{i,t+s}}  \tag{2.6}\]
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
% \[\max_{P^*, \{\Omega_ij \}_{j=0}^N}\ \mathbb{E}_t\sum_{s=0}^\infty(1-\delta_i)^s\Lambda_{t,t+s}\left[P^*-\frac{MC_{i,t+s}}{\mathcal{M}}\right]\left(\frac{P^*}{P_{i,t+s}}\right)^{-\theta} e^{\varepsilon_{i, t+s}+a_i(\Omega_i)}\,\zeta_i\prod_{j=0}^{N}X_{ifj, t+s}^{\Omega_{ij}}.\]
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

% \[\mathbb{E}_t\sum_{s=0}^\infty(1-\delta_i)^s\Lambda_{t,t+s}P_{i,t+s}^{\theta}Y_{i,t+s}\left[P^*-\mathcal{M}\frac{MC_{i,t+s}}{\mathcal{M}}\right]=0\;\Longrightarrow\;\mathbb{E}_t\sum_{s=0}^\infty(1-\delta_i)^s\Lambda_{t,t+s}P_{i,t+s}^{\theta}Y_{i,t+s}\left[P^*_{it}-MC_{i,t+s}\right]=0.\]
The reset price is a weighted average of current and expected future
\emph{pre-subsidy} marginal costs; the markup and the subsidy cancel
exactly (M2). This is what makes the flexible-price limit
(\(\delta_i\to1\)) efficient: \(P_{it}=MC_{it}\).

By the law of large numbers across producers, the sectoral index evolves
as
\[P_{it}^{1-\theta}=(1-\delta_i)P_{i,t-1}^{1-\theta}+\delta_i(P^*_{it})^{1-\theta}. \tag{2.7}\]

\textbf{(c) Technique choice.} At the start of the regime, producer
\(f\) chooses
\(\Omega_{if}\in\{\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_+:\mathbf{1}^\top\Omega=1\}\)
to maximize the expected discounted value of its profit stream, taking
as given the sectoral price \(P_{it}\), sectoral demand \(Y_{it}\) and
the discount factor. Under I1--I2 this value is evaluated at the
flexible-price distribution, in which the producer resets its price
every period. Solved in Section 4.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{2.3 The union (sector
\(0\))}{2.3 The union (sector 0)}}\label{the-union-sector-0}

Union member \(f\) buys raw hours from the household at \(W^h_t\) and
sells differentiated labor services at price \(W_{ft}\equiv P_{0ft}\).
Firms' labor demand aggregates the varieties with the same CES
elasticity \(\theta\), so
\(W_t\equiv P_{0t}=\left(\int W_{ft}^{1-\theta}df\right)^{1/(1-\theta)}\)
is the wage index paid by producers. Under T4 the union's marginal cost
is \(MC_{0t}=W^h_t\), i.e.~\(mc_{0t}=w^h_t\), and by (2.2)
\[mc_{0t}=p^C_t+\gamma c_t+\phi l_t. \tag{2.8}\] The union member's
reset problem is (2.6) with \(i=0\) and reset probability
\(\delta_0=\delta_L\). Sector \(0\) is therefore formally identical to
any other sector: it has a Calvo Phillips curve, a subsidy that offsets
its markup, and a marginal cost given by (2.8) instead of (2.5). In the
notation of Section 5, sector \(0\) is the only sector with a nonzero
direct labor share: \(\alpha\equiv e_0\), and all other sectors' labor
shares appear in column \(0\) of \(\Omega\).

\subsubsection{2.4 Government}\label{government}

The government pays the subsidy \(\tau\sum_{i}\sum_jP_{jt}X_{ijt}\) and
levies \(T_t\) on the household so that its budget balances period by
period: \(T_t=\tau\sum_i\sum_jP_{jt}X_{ijt}\). It issues no debt.

\subsubsection{2.5 Central bank}\label{central-bank}

Under G1 the central bank chooses \(i_t\) such that
\(\tilde y_t\equiv y_t-y^{nat}_t=0\) for all \(t\). Nothing else about
the rule is needed in Section 5; R23 Lemma 4 supplies existence,
uniqueness and an implementing Taylor rule. The Euler equation (2.3) is
then needed only to back out \(i_t\).

\subsubsection{2.6 Market clearing}\label{market-clearing}

Goods: \(Y_{it}=C_{it}+\sum_{j=1}^N\int_0^1X_{jfit}\,df\) for \(i\ge1\).
Labor services: \(Y_{0t}=\sum_{j=1}^N\int_0^1X_{jf0t}\,df\). Raw hours:
\(L_t=\int_0^1Y_{0ft}\,df\). Bonds: \(B_t=0\).

\subsubsection{2.7 Equilibrium}\label{equilibrium}

Given a regime \((\mu,\Sigma)\), an equilibrium is a network
\(\Omega^*\) and stochastic processes for prices, quantities and the
interest rate such that (i) each producer's technique solves 2.2(c)
given the flex-price distribution induced by \(\Omega^*\) and the other
producers' techniques; (ii) each period, given \(\Omega^*\) and realized
\(\varepsilon_t\), input choices solve 2.2(a), reset prices solve (2.6),
sectoral indices follow (2.7), the household solves 2.1, the government
budget balances, the central bank implements G1, and markets clear.
Under I2 the equilibrium is computed recursively: Section 4 solves (i)
using the exact flex-price equilibrium of Section 3, and Section 5
solves (ii) to first order around it.

\subsection{3. The flexible-price equilibrium at a given network
(exact)}\label{the-flexible-price-equilibrium-at-a-given-network-exact}

Fix a network \(\Omega\) and suppose every producer can reset its price
every period (\(\delta_i=1\) for all \(i\)). Then (2.6) gives
\(P_{it}=MC_{it}\) for all \(i\), including \(W_t=W^h_t\). Everything
below holds exactly, not to first order, because of P2, T1, T4 and M2.
Time subscripts are dropped; the period is static.

\subsubsection{3.1 Prices}\label{prices}

Write (2.5) and \(mc_0=w^h\) in vector form. Let \(\log A\) be the
vector with entries \(\varepsilon_i+a_i(\Omega_i)\) for \(i\ge1\) and
\(0\) for \(i=0\), and let \(\alpha\equiv e_0\). Then
\[p=\Omega p+\alpha w^h-\log A .\] Row \(0\) of \(\Omega\) is zero (T4),
so row \(0\) reads \(p_0=w=w^h\). The Leontief inverse
\(L\equiv(I-\Omega)^{-1}\) exists because \(\Omega\) is substochastic
with a zero row and every sector reaches sector \(0\) through a chain of
positive shares. Hence \[p=L\alpha\,w^h-L\log A. \tag{3.1}\] Because
each row of \(\Omega\) sums to \(1-\alpha_i\) (T1, T4),
\((I-\Omega)\mathbf{1}=\alpha\), hence \(L\alpha=\mathbf{1}\), and (3.1)
becomes \[p_i=w-\big[L\log A\big]_i\qquad\text{for all }i. \tag{3.2}\]
Every price equals the wage minus the cumulated productivity of the
sector's own supply chain. Prices in wage units are linear in \(\log A\)
and therefore, given E3, Gaussian. This is KMNT Lemma 1 with the union
as sector \(0\).

\subsubsection{3.2 Sales, Domar weights and
GDP}\label{sales-domar-weights-and-gdp}

Sales of sector \(i\) equal final demand plus intermediate demand from
all sectors. Using (2.1), the factor demands in 2.2(a), and
\(MC_j=P_j\): \[P_iY_i=\beta_iP^CC+\sum_{j}\Omega_{ji}P_jY_j .\] In
vector form with \(s_i\equiv P_iY_i\): \(s=\beta\,P^CC+\Omega^\top s\),
so \(s^\top=\beta^\top L\,P^CC\). Define the \textbf{Domar weights}
\[\lambda^\top\equiv\beta^\top L=\beta^\top(I-\Omega)^{-1},\qquad P_iY_i=\lambda_i\,P^CC. \tag{3.3}\]
For the union, \(\lambda_0=\beta^\top L e_0\). Post-multiplying
\(L\alpha=\mathbf{1}\) by \(\beta^\top\) gives
\(\lambda^\top\alpha=\lambda_0=1\): union sales equal nominal GDP,
\(WL=P^CC\). Nominal GDP is the wage bill; profits net of the subsidy
are zero because \(P_i=MC_i\) and the subsidy is rebated (M2). Hence
\[p^C=\beta^\top p=w-\lambda^\top\log A,\qquad c=w+l-p^C=l+\lambda^\top\log A. \tag{3.4}\]
Define aggregate log TFP
\[z\equiv\lambda^\top\log A=\sum_{i=1}^N\lambda_i\big(\varepsilon_i+a_i(\Omega_i)\big). \tag{3.5}\]
Then \(c=l+z\) exactly: real GDP is aggregate TFP times hours. This is
Hulten's theorem holding exactly (not just to first order) because all
shares are constant.

\subsubsection{3.3 Hours, output and the natural
allocation}\label{hours-output-and-the-natural-allocation}

Combine (3.4) with the labor supply condition (2.2),
\(w^h-p^C=\gamma c+\phi l\), and \(w=w^h\): the real wage is
\(w-p^C=z\), so \(z=\gamma c+\phi l=\gamma(l+z)+\phi l\), giving
\[l^{nat}=\frac{1-\gamma}{\gamma+\phi}\,z,\qquad c^{nat}=y^{nat}=\frac{1+\phi}{\gamma+\phi}\,z. \tag{3.6}\]
These are R23 Lemma 1 with \(\log A\) replaced by the exact TFP vector.
Under P3 (\(\gamma>1\)) hours fall when productivity rises: the income
effect dominates. The steady state of the regime is (3.2)--(3.6)
evaluated at \(\log A=\mu+a(\Omega^*)\) (with \(\varepsilon_0=a_0=0\)),
and the sticky-price block linearizes around it (N2).

\subsection{4. The network block}\label{the-network-block}

\subsubsection{4.1 What a producer
maximizes}\label{what-a-producer-maximizes}

Under I1--I2, producer \(f\) in sector \(i\) evaluates a candidate
technique \(\Omega'_i\) by the expected discounted profit it would earn
if it used \(\Omega'_i\) while resetting its price every period, in an
economy where all other producers use the equilibrium network
\(\Omega^*\). Two facts simplify this.

First, a producer with pre-subsidy marginal cost \(MC'\) and a flexible
price charges the markup \(\mathcal{M}\) over its effective marginal
cost \(MC'/\mathcal{M}\), so \(P_f=MC'\). Its profit per period is
\[\Pi_f=\left(P_f-\frac{MC'}{\mathcal{M}}\right)\left(\frac{P_f}{P_i}\right)^{-\theta}Y_i=\left(1-\frac1{\mathcal{M}}\right)MC'\left(\frac{MC'}{P_i}\right)^{-\theta}Y_i=\frac{1}{\theta}\left(\frac{MC'}{P_i}\right)^{1-\theta}P_iY_i . \tag{4.1}\]

Second, by (3.3), \(P_iY_i=\lambda_iP^CC\) and the state price is
\(\Lambda_t=\rho^tC_t^{-\gamma}/P^C_t\), so
\[\Lambda_tP_{it}Y_{it}=\rho^t\lambda_iC_t^{1-\gamma}. \tag{4.2}\] The
nominal-GDP scaling and the price level cancel: what remains is real
consumption raised to \(1-\gamma\). Therefore the value of the technique
is
\[\mathcal{J}_i(\Omega'_i)=\frac{\lambda_i}{\theta}\sum_t\rho^t\,\mathbb{E}\left[C_t^{1-\gamma}\left(\frac{MC_{it}(\Omega'_i)}{P_{it}}\right)^{1-\theta}\right]. \tag{4.3}\]
Under I1 every period has the same unconditional distribution, so the
\(\rho^t\) sum is a constant multiple of a single-period expectation and
can be dropped. Write the bracket in logs. From (2.5) and (3.6),
\[\log\left[C^{1-\gamma}\left(\frac{MC_i(\Omega'_i)}{P_i}\right)^{1-\theta}\right]=(1-\gamma)c+(1-\theta)\Big[\Omega_i'^{\top}p-\varepsilon_i-a_i(\Omega'_i)-p_i\Big],\]
which is Gaussian because \(c\) and \(p\) are linear in \(\varepsilon\)
(Section 3). Define the log \textbf{effective discount factor}
\[\tilde\lambda\equiv(1-\gamma)\,c^{nat}=-\Gamma z,\qquad\Gamma\equiv\frac{(\gamma-1)(1+\phi)}{\gamma+\phi}, \tag{4.4}\]
using (3.6). Using
\(\mathbb{E}[e^X]=\exp(\mathbb{E}X+\tfrac12\mathbb{V}X)\) for Gaussian
\(X\), the log of the expectation in (4.3) is
\[\log\mathcal{J}_i(\Omega'_i)=\text{const}+(1-\theta)\Big[\Omega_i'^{\top}\mathbb{E}p-a_i(\Omega'_i)\Big]+\tfrac12\mathbb{V}\Big[\tilde\lambda+(1-\theta)\big(\Omega_i'^{\top}p-\varepsilon_i-a_i(\Omega'_i)-p_i\big)\Big], \tag{4.5}\]
where the constant collects terms that do not depend on \(\Omega'_i\),
including \(\mathbb{E}[\tilde\lambda-(1-\theta)(\varepsilon_i+p_i)]\).

\subsubsection{4.2 First-order condition and the risk-adjusted
price}\label{first-order-condition-and-the-risk-adjusted-price}

Differentiate (4.5) with respect to \(\Omega'_{ik}\). The deterministic
part gives
\((1-\theta)(\mathbb{E}p_k-\partial a_i/\partial\Omega_{ik})\). The
variance part gives
\(\tfrac12\cdot2\,\mathrm{Cov}\big[\tilde\lambda+(1-\theta)(\Omega_i'^{\top}p-\varepsilon_i-a_i-p_i),\,(1-\theta)p_k\big]\),
since only \(p_k\) is stochastic in
\(\partial(\cdot)/\partial\Omega'_{ik}\). Collecting and dividing
through by \((1-\theta)\ne0\), with multiplier \(\nu_i\) on
\(\mathbf{1}^\top\Omega'_i=1\):
\[\mathbb{E}p_k-\frac{\partial a_i}{\partial\Omega_{ik}}+\mathrm{Cov}[\tilde\lambda,p_k]+(1-\theta)\,\mathrm{Cov}\big[\Omega_i'^{\top}p-\varepsilon_i-a_i(\Omega'_i)-p_i,\;p_k\big]-\nu_i=0. \tag{4.6}\]
Now impose symmetry: in equilibrium \(\Omega'_i=\Omega^*_i\), so
\(\Omega_i'^{\top}p-\varepsilon_i-a_i(\Omega'_i)=mc_i=p_i\) by (2.5) and
Section 3. The last covariance in (4.6) is therefore
\(\mathrm{Cov}[p_i-p_i,p_k]=0\) \textbf{at the equilibrium technique},
and the condition collapses to
\[\frac{\partial a_i(\Omega^*_i)}{\partial\Omega_{ik}}=R_k-\nu_i\quad\text{for all }k,\qquad R\equiv\mathbb{E}[p]+\mathrm{Cov}[p,\tilde\lambda]. \tag{4.7}\]
\(R\) is the vector of \textbf{risk-adjusted prices}: the expected price
plus its covariance with the household's effective marginal valuation.
This is KMNT Lemma 2 with two changes. (i) The producer is a monopolist,
not a price-taker; the CES structure adds the last term of (4.6), which
vanishes only at the symmetric equilibrium, so (4.7) is a statement
about the equilibrium first-order condition, not about the objective.
(ii) The discount-factor weight is \(\Gamma\) instead of \(\gamma-1\),
because elastic labor scales sales with \(C^{1-\gamma}\) rather than
with \(C^{-\gamma}\) alone (equation (4.2)).

Using (3.2) with \(w=0\) (wage numeraire) and (3.5),
\[\mathbb{E}p=-L(\mu+a(\Omega^*)),\qquad\mathrm{Cov}[p,\tilde\lambda]=-\Gamma\,\mathrm{Cov}[-L\log A,\;\lambda^\top\log A]=\Gamma\,L\Sigma L^\top\beta, \tag{4.8}\]
where \(\Sigma\) is padded with a zero row and column for sector \(0\)
and \(\lambda=L^\top\beta\). So \(R_0=0\) and, for \(k\ge1\), \(R_k\) is
the expected price of \(k\) in wage units plus \(\Gamma\) times the
covariance of \(k\)'s price with aggregate TFP. A supplier whose price
rises when the economy is poor (\(\mathrm{Cov}[p_k,z]<0\),
i.e.~\([L\Sigma\lambda]_k>0\)) is penalized in proportion to \(\Gamma\).

\subsubsection{4.3 Second-order condition}\label{second-order-condition}

The Hessian of (4.5) with respect to \(\Omega'_i\) is
\((1-\theta)(-H_i)+(1-\theta)^2\Sigma_p\), where
\(\Sigma_p\equiv\mathbb{V}[p]=L\Sigma L^\top\). For a maximum it must be
negative definite on the tangent space of the simplex; dividing by
\(\theta-1>0\),
\[H_i+(\theta-1)\,\Sigma_p\prec0\quad\text{on }\{v:\mathbf{1}^\top v=0\}. \tag{4.9}\]
The second term is positive semidefinite: a monopolist's profit (4.1) is
convex in its own cost, so cost variance \emph{raises} expected profit
with strength \(\theta-1\). Condition (4.9) says the curvature of the
TFP shifter must beat this convexity. KMNT's price-taking producer has
objective Hessian \(-H_i+\Sigma_p\succ0\) for a minimum and needs no
such condition.

\subsubsection{4.4 Closed form under T2}\label{closed-form-under-t2}

With quadratic \(a_i\),
\(\partial a_i/\partial\Omega_i=H_i(\Omega_i-\Omega_i^\circ)\) and (4.7)
reads \(H_i(\Omega^*_i-\Omega^\circ_i)=R-\nu_i\mathbf{1}\), so
\(\Omega_i^*=\Omega_i^\circ+H_i^{-1}(R-\nu_i\mathbf{1})\). Imposing
\(\mathbf{1}^\top\Omega_i^*=1=\mathbf{1}^\top\Omega_i^\circ\) gives
\(\mathbf{1}^\top H_i^{-1}(R-\nu_i\mathbf{1})=0\), so
\(\nu_i=\mathbf{1}^\top H_i^{-1}R/\mathbf{1}^\top H_i^{-1}\mathbf{1}\)
and
\[\Omega_i^*=\Omega_i^\circ+\hat H_i^{-1}R,\qquad\hat H_i^{-1}\equiv H_i^{-1}-\frac{H_i^{-1}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top H_i^{-1}}{\mathbf{1}^\top H_i^{-1}\mathbf{1}}. \tag{4.10}\]
\(\hat H_i^{-1}\) annihilates \(\mathbf{1}\), so only \emph{relative}
risk-adjusted prices move the technique; adding a constant to \(R\)
changes nothing, as it should under a numeraire choice. The equilibrium
network is the fixed point
\(\Omega^*=\Omega^\circ+\hat H^{-1}R(\Omega^*)\) with \(R(\Omega^*)\)
from (4.8).

\subsubsection{4.5 Planner equivalence and the risk
weight}\label{planner-equivalence-and-the-risk-weight}

The flex-price equilibrium is undistorted (M2, M3), so by the first
welfare theorem the equilibrium network maximizes expected household
utility. Using \(C=Z^{(1+\phi)/(\gamma+\phi)}\) and
\(L=Z^{(1-\gamma)/(\gamma+\phi)}\) from (3.6), indirect utility as a
function of \(Z=e^z\) is
\[V(Z)=\frac{Z^{k}}{1-\gamma}-\frac{Z^{k}}{1+\phi}=\frac{Z^k}{k},\qquad k\equiv\frac{(1-\gamma)(1+\phi)}{\gamma+\phi}=-\Gamma ,\]
since
\(\frac1{1-\gamma}-\frac1{1+\phi}=\frac{\gamma+\phi}{(1-\gamma)(1+\phi)}\).
With \(z\) Gaussian,
\(\mathbb{E}V=\frac1k\exp(k\mathbb{E}z+\tfrac12k^2\mathbb{V}z)\). For
\(\gamma>1\), \(k<0\) and \(1/k<0\), so maximizing \(\mathbb{E}V\) is
minimizing \(k\mathbb{E}z+\tfrac12k^2\mathbb{V}z\), i.e.~(dividing by
\(k<0\)) maximizing
\[\mathcal{W}(\Omega)=\mathbb{E}[z]-\tfrac12\,\Gamma\,\mathbb{V}[z]=\lambda^\top\big(\mu+a(\Omega)\big)-\tfrac12\,\Gamma\,\lambda^\top\Sigma\lambda. \tag{4.11}\]
The same expression results for \(\gamma<1\) (then \(k>0\), \(1/k>0\),
and \(\Gamma<0\): the planner rewards variance) and for \(\gamma=1\) by
the log limit (\(\Gamma=0\)). This is KMNT's planner objective with
\(\rho_{KMNT}-1\) replaced by \(\Gamma\); as \(\phi\to\infty\),
\(\Gamma\to\gamma-1\). KMNT Prop. 1 shows the fixed point of
(4.7)--(4.8) is the maximizer of (4.11); the proof is unchanged by the
substitution, and the derivation in 4.1--4.2 verifies its key step ---
that producers discount with weight \(\Gamma\) --- directly from the
decentralized problem.

\subsubsection{4.6 Comparative statics
(inherited)}\label{comparative-statics-inherited}

Everything in KMNT Sections 5--7 applies with \(\rho-1\to\Gamma\): Prop.
2 (own Domar weight falls in own variance), Prop. 3
(\(d\lambda/d\gamma=-H^{-1}\partial\mathcal{E}/\partial\gamma\) for any
belief parameter \(\gamma\in\{\mu_i,\Sigma_{ij}\}\), with
\(\mathcal{E}\equiv\mu-\Gamma\Sigma\lambda\)), Cor. 3 (sign of
cross-effects given by global complementarity), and Lemma 9 (with
\(H_i=-\bar\kappa\hat H_i\),
\(\Omega_i^*=\Omega_i^\circ+\bar\kappa^{-1}(\hat H_i^\circ)^{-1}R^\circ+O(\bar\kappa^{-2})\)).

\subsection{5. The sticky-price block}\label{the-sticky-price-block}

From here on all variables are log-deviations from the regime's steady
state (N2), so \(\log A_t\equiv\varepsilon_t-\mu\) with
\(\log A_{0t}=0\), and \(a_i(\Omega_i^*)\) is absorbed into the steady
state. \(\Omega\equiv\Omega^*\), \(L\equiv(I-\Omega^*)^{-1}\),
\(\lambda\equiv\lambda^*\) throughout.

\subsubsection{5.1 From the Calvo problem to the sectoral Phillips
curve}\label{from-the-calvo-problem-to-the-sectoral-phillips-curve}

\textbf{Reset price.} Write
\(x_{t,t+s}\equiv\Lambda_{t,t+s}P_{i,t+s}^{\theta}Y_{i,t+s}\) for the
weight in (2.6). In the zero-inflation steady state \(P^*=P=MC\) and
\(x_{t,t+s}=\rho^s\bar x\). Because the bracket
\([P^*_{it}-MC_{i,t+s}]\) is zero in steady state, first-order
deviations of \(x_{t,t+s}\) multiply zero and drop out; only the
bracket's own deviation survives. Dividing (2.6) by \(\bar x\bar P\),
\[\sum_{s=0}^\infty(1-\delta_i)^s\rho^s\,\mathbb{E}_t\big[p^*_{it}-mc_{i,t+s}\big]=0\;\Longrightarrow\;p^*_{it}=\big(1-\rho(1-\delta_i)\big)\sum_{s=0}^\infty\big(\rho(1-\delta_i)\big)^s\mathbb{E}_t\,mc_{i,t+s}, \tag{5.1}\]
using \(\sum_s(\rho(1-\delta_i))^s=1/(1-\rho(1-\delta_i))\).
Equivalently, splitting off \(s=0\),
\[p^*_{it}=\big(1-\rho(1-\delta_i)\big)mc_{it}+\rho(1-\delta_i)\,\mathbb{E}_tp^*_{i,t+1}. \tag{5.2}\]

\textbf{Price index.} Log-linearizing (2.7):
\((1-\theta)p_{it}=(1-\delta_i)(1-\theta)p_{i,t-1}+\delta_i(1-\theta)p^*_{it}\),
so
\[p_{it}=(1-\delta_i)p_{i,t-1}+\delta_ip^*_{it}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\pi_{it}=\delta_i\big(p^*_{it}-p_{i,t-1}\big). \tag{5.3}\]

\textbf{Combining.} Subtract \(p_{i,t-1}\) from both sides of (5.2):
\[p^*_{it}-p_{i,t-1}=\big(1-\rho(1-\delta_i)\big)\big(mc_{it}-p_{i,t-1}\big)+\rho(1-\delta_i)\big(\mathbb{E}_tp^*_{i,t+1}-p_{i,t-1}\big).\]
Use \(mc_{it}-p_{i,t-1}=(mc_{it}-p_{it})+\pi_{it}\) and, from (5.3)
shifted forward,
\(\mathbb{E}_tp^*_{i,t+1}-p_{i,t-1}=(\mathbb{E}_tp^*_{i,t+1}-p_{it})+\pi_{it}=\mathbb{E}_t\pi_{i,t+1}/\delta_i+\pi_{it}\).
Then multiply through by \(\delta_i\) and use (5.3) on the left:
\[\pi_{it}=\delta_i\big(1-\rho(1-\delta_i)\big)\big(mc_{it}-p_{it}\big)+\delta_i\big[(1-\rho(1-\delta_i))+\rho(1-\delta_i)\big]\pi_{it}+\rho(1-\delta_i)\mathbb{E}_t\pi_{i,t+1}.\]
The middle bracket equals \(1\), so
\(\pi_{it}(1-\delta_i)=\delta_i(1-\rho(1-\delta_i))(mc_{it}-p_{it})+\rho(1-\delta_i)\mathbb{E}_t\pi_{i,t+1}\).
Dividing by \(1-\delta_i\):
\[\boxed{\;\pi_{it}=\rho\,\mathbb{E}_t\pi_{i,t+1}+\kappa_i\big(mc_{it}-p_{it}\big),\qquad\kappa_i\equiv\frac{\delta_i\big(1-\rho(1-\delta_i)\big)}{1-\delta_i}\;} \tag{5.4}\]
This holds for every sector \(i\in\{0,\dots,N\}\), with \(mc_{0t}\)
given by (2.8). In matrix form,
\(K\equiv\mathrm{diag}(\kappa_0,\dots,\kappa_N)\):
\[\pi_t=\rho\,\mathbb{E}_t\pi_{t+1}+K\big(mc_t-p_t\big). \tag{5.5}\]

\subsubsection{5.2 Marginal-cost gaps in terms of prices, productivity
and the output
gap}\label{marginal-cost-gaps-in-terms-of-prices-productivity-and-the-output-gap}

Stack (2.5) for \(i\ge1\) and (2.8) for \(i=0\):
\[mc_t=\Omega p_t+\alpha\,w^h_t-\log A_t,\qquad w^h_t=p^C_t+\gamma c_t+\phi l_t . \tag{5.6}\]
Three first-order facts convert \(w^h_t\) into observables. (a)
Goods-market clearing with no investment: \(c_t=y_t\). (b) Definition of
the gap: \(y_t=y^{nat}_t+\tilde y_t\). (c) \emph{Output gap equals hours
gap}: aggregate output is
\(y_t=\lambda^\top\log A_t+l_t+O(\|\cdot\|^2)\) under sticky prices as
well as flexible ones, because the steady state is efficient (M2) and by
the envelope theorem the misallocation of inputs across sectors caused
by relative-price distortions has no first-order effect on aggregate
output (R23 Remark 5). Subtracting the natural counterpart,
\(\tilde y_t=\tilde l_t\). Therefore
\[\gamma c_t+\phi l_t=\gamma\big(y^{nat}_t+\tilde y_t\big)+\phi\big(l^{nat}_t+\tilde y_t\big)=\frac{\gamma(1+\phi)+\phi(1-\gamma)}{\gamma+\phi}\lambda^\top\log A_t+(\gamma+\phi)\tilde y_t=\lambda^\top\log A_t+(\gamma+\phi)\tilde y_t ,\]
using (3.6) and \(\gamma(1+\phi)+\phi(1-\gamma)=\gamma+\phi\). So
\[w^h_t=\beta^\top p_t+\lambda^\top\log A_t+(\gamma+\phi)\tilde y_t . \tag{5.7}\]
The natural real wage rises one-for-one with aggregate TFP; the gap adds
\((\gamma+\phi)\tilde y_t\). Substituting (5.7) into (5.6) and
subtracting \(p_t\):
\[mc_t-p_t=-\big[(I-\Omega)-\alpha\beta^\top\big]p_t-\big[I-\alpha\lambda^\top\big]\log A_t+\alpha(\gamma+\phi)\tilde y_t . \tag{5.8}\]
Two identities collapse the two brackets into one operator. From
\((I-\Omega)\mathbf{1}=\alpha\):
\[(I-\Omega)-\alpha\beta^\top=(I-\Omega)\big(I-\mathbf{1}\beta^\top\big)\equiv Q ,\]
and from \(\lambda^\top=\beta^\top L\), \((I-\Omega)L=I\):
\[I-\alpha\lambda^\top=(I-\Omega)L-(I-\Omega)\mathbf{1}\beta^\top L=(I-\Omega)\big(I-\mathbf{1}\beta^\top\big)L=QL .\]
Hence
\[\boxed{\;mc_t-p_t=-\,Q\big(p_t+L\log A_t\big)+\alpha(\gamma+\phi)\tilde y_t\;} \tag{5.9}\]
Interpretation. By (3.2) the natural price vector is
\(p^{nat}_t=\mathbf{1}w_t-L\log A_t\), and
\(Q\mathbf{1}=(I-\Omega)(\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{1})=0\), so
\(Q(p_t+L\log A_t)=Q(p_t-p^{nat}_t)\): the marginal-cost gap of sector
\(i\) is minus the gap between actual and natural \emph{relative}
prices, weighted by \(i\)'s input shares, plus (for the union only) the
labor-cost gap \((\gamma+\phi)\tilde y_t\). Following R23, define the
state
\[\chi_t\equiv p_{t-1}+L\log A_t,\qquad p_t+L\log A_t=\chi_t+\pi_t . \tag{5.10}\]

\subsubsection{5.3 The sectoral Phillips-curve system and its map to
R23}\label{the-sectoral-phillips-curve-system-and-its-map-to-r23}

Substitute (5.9)--(5.10) into (5.5):
\[\pi_t=\rho\,\mathbb{E}_t\pi_{t+1}+K\alpha(\gamma+\phi)\tilde y_t-KQ\big(\chi_t+\pi_t\big)\;\Longrightarrow\;(I+KQ)\pi_t=\rho\,\mathbb{E}_t\pi_{t+1}+K\alpha(\gamma+\phi)\tilde y_t-KQ\chi_t .\]
Let \(M\equiv(I+KQ)^{-1}\) and define
\[V\equiv MKQ=I-M,\qquad B\equiv MK\alpha . \tag{5.11}\] (\(V=I-M\)
because \(I-(I+KQ)^{-1}=(I+KQ)^{-1}(I+KQ-I)=(I+KQ)^{-1}KQ\).) Then
\[\boxed{\;\pi_t=\rho\,(I-V)\,\mathbb{E}_t\pi_{t+1}+B(\gamma+\phi)\tilde y_t-V\chi_t,\qquad p_t=p_{t-1}+\pi_t\;} \tag{5.12}\]
This is R23 Proposition 2. The state vector is \((p_{t-1},\log A_t)\)
with \(\log A_t=\eta\log A_{t-1}+\xi_t\).

\textbf{Map to R23's rigidity matrix.} R23 writes \(V\) and \(B\) in
terms of \(\Delta=\mathrm{diag}(\hat\delta_i)\). Define
\(\hat\delta_i\equiv\kappa_i/(1+\kappa_i)\), so
\(K=\Delta(I-\Delta)^{-1}\). Explicitly,
\[\hat\delta_i=\frac{\delta_i(1-\rho(1-\delta_i))}{(1-\delta_i)+\delta_i(1-\rho(1-\delta_i))}=\frac{\delta_i\big(1-\rho(1-\delta_i)\big)}{1-\rho\,\delta_i(1-\delta_i)}, \tag{5.13}\]
which is R23's \(\hat\delta_i(\delta_i,\rho)\);
\(\hat\delta_i\in(0,1)\), \(\hat\delta_i\to1\) as \(\delta_i\to1\) and
\(\hat\delta_i\to0\) as \(\delta_i\to0\). Now
\[I+KQ=(I-\Delta)^{-1}\big[(I-\Delta)+\Delta Q\big]=(I-\Delta)^{-1}\big[I-\Delta+\Delta(I-\Omega)-\Delta\alpha\beta^\top\big]=(I-\Delta)^{-1}\big[I-\Delta\Omega-\Delta\alpha\beta^\top\big],\]
so \(M=\big[I-\Delta\Omega-\Delta\alpha\beta^\top\big]^{-1}(I-\Delta)\).
Apply the Sherman--Morrison identity
\([G-uv^\top]^{-1}=G^{-1}+G^{-1}uv^\top G^{-1}/(1-v^\top G^{-1}u)\) with
\(G=I-\Delta\Omega\), \(u=\Delta\alpha\), \(v=\beta\), and define
\[\delta^{\beta}\equiv\beta^\top(I-\Delta\Omega)^{-1}\Delta\ \ (\text{row vector}),\qquad\delta^{\beta}\alpha\equiv\beta^\top(I-\Delta\Omega)^{-1}\Delta\alpha\ \ (\text{scalar}),\qquad B\equiv\frac{(I-\Delta\Omega)^{-1}\Delta\alpha}{1-\delta^{\beta}\alpha}. \tag{5.14}\]
Then
\([I-\Delta\Omega-\Delta\alpha\beta^\top]^{-1}=(I-\Delta\Omega)^{-1}+B\beta^\top(I-\Delta\Omega)^{-1}\),
and:

\emph{The gap coefficient.}
\(MK\alpha=[I-\Delta\Omega-\Delta\alpha\beta^\top]^{-1}(I-\Delta)\Delta(I-\Delta)^{-1}\alpha=[(I-\Delta\Omega)^{-1}+B\beta^\top(I-\Delta\Omega)^{-1}]\Delta\alpha=(I-\Delta\Omega)^{-1}\Delta\alpha\Big(1+\frac{\delta^\beta\alpha}{1-\delta^\beta\alpha}\Big)=B\),
confirming that the \(B\) of (5.11) is the \(B\) of (5.14).

\emph{The cost-push matrix.}
\(V=MKQ=[(I-\Delta\Omega)^{-1}+B\beta^\top(I-\Delta\Omega)^{-1}]\Delta\big[(I-\Omega)-\alpha\beta^\top\big]\).
Expanding the four products,
\[V=(I-\Delta\Omega)^{-1}\Delta(I-\Omega)-(I-\Delta\Omega)^{-1}\Delta\alpha\,\beta^\top+B\,\delta^\beta(I-\Omega)-B\,(\delta^\beta\alpha)\,\beta^\top .\]
The second term is \(-B(1-\delta^\beta\alpha)\beta^\top\) by (5.14);
adding the fourth gives \(-B\beta^\top=-B\lambda^\top(I-\Omega)\) since
\(\beta^\top=\lambda^\top(I-\Omega)\). Therefore
\[V=\Big[(I-\Delta\Omega)^{-1}\Delta-B\big(\lambda^\top-\delta^\beta\big)\Big](I-\Omega), \tag{5.15}\]
which is R23's expression. The operator \((I-\Delta\Omega)^{-1}\Delta\)
is the rigidity-discounted Leontief inverse: entry \((i,j)\) sums over
all supply-chain paths from \(i\) back to \(j\), each step discounted by
the reset probability of the sector at that step.

\subsubsection{5.4 Closing the output gap
(G1)}\label{closing-the-output-gap-g1}

Under G1 the gap term in (5.12) is identically zero:
\[\pi_t=\rho\,(I-V)\,\mathbb{E}_t\pi_{t+1}-V\chi_t . \tag{5.16}\] R23
Lemma 4 establishes that, with \(\delta_i<1\) for all \(i\) (N1) and
AR(1) productivity (E3), (5.16) together with (5.10) has a unique
bounded solution, and that it is implemented by a Taylor rule on the
divine-coincidence index. All network effects on inflation under G1 run
through \(V\) and \(\chi_t\); the slope \(B\) is switched off by
construction.

\subsubsection{5.5 Inflation indices, slopes and cost-push
residuals}\label{inflation-indices-slopes-and-cost-push-residuals}

For any weight vector \(\varphi\) with \(\varphi^\top\mathbf{1}=1\),
define \(\pi^{\varphi}_t\equiv\varphi^\top\pi_t\) and
\(\delta^{\varphi}\equiv\varphi^\top(I-\Delta\Omega)^{-1}\Delta\).
Pre-multiplying (5.12) by \(\varphi^\top\):
\[\pi^{\varphi}_t=\rho\,\varphi^\top(I-V)\mathbb{E}_t\pi_{t+1}+\kappa^{\varphi}(\gamma+\phi)\tilde y_t+u^{\varphi}_t , \tag{5.17}\]
with
\[\kappa^{\varphi}\equiv\varphi^\top B=\frac{\delta^{\varphi}\alpha}{1-\delta^{\beta}\alpha},\qquad u^{\varphi}_t\equiv-\varphi^\top V\chi_t=\Big[\kappa^{\varphi}\big(\lambda^\top-\delta^{\beta}\big)-\delta^{\varphi}\Big](I-\Omega)\chi_t , \tag{5.18}\]
the second by pre-multiplying (5.15) by \(-\varphi^\top\). This is R23
Corollary 2. The expectation term is not proportional to
\(\mathbb{E}_t\pi^\varphi_{t+1}\) in general, so (5.17) is not a closed
aggregate Phillips curve.

For the CPI (\(\varphi=\beta\)),
\(\kappa^C=\delta^\beta\alpha/(1-\delta^\beta\alpha)\) and the bracket
in (5.18) simplifies:
\[\frac{\delta^\beta\alpha(\lambda^\top-\delta^\beta)}{1-\delta^\beta\alpha}-\delta^\beta=\frac{\delta^\beta\alpha\,\lambda^\top-\delta^\beta\alpha\,\delta^\beta-\delta^\beta+\delta^\beta\alpha\,\delta^\beta}{1-\delta^\beta\alpha}=\frac{\delta^\beta\alpha\,\lambda^\top-\delta^\beta}{1-\delta^\beta\alpha},\]
so
\[u^C_t=\frac{\delta^{\beta}\alpha\,\lambda^\top-\delta^{\beta}}{1-\delta^{\beta}\alpha}\,(I-\Omega)\chi_t . \tag{5.19}\]
On impact of a shock with \(p_{t-1}=0\), \((I-\Omega)\chi_t=\log A_t\),
so \(u^C_t=v^C\log A_t\) with the \textbf{exposure vector}
\[v^C\equiv\frac{\delta^{\beta}\alpha\,\lambda^\top-\delta^{\beta}}{1-\delta^{\beta}\alpha}. \tag{5.20}\]
Entry \(j\) is the impact cost-push in CPI from a unit productivity
shock in \(j\): the wage channel \(\delta^\beta\alpha\,\lambda_j\)
(wages fall with aggregate TFP, discounted by wage and price rigidity
along every path) minus the direct channel \(\delta^\beta_j\) (the
rigidity-discounted exposure of consumption to \(j\)'s price), scaled by
the wage--price multiplier \(1/(1-\delta^\beta\alpha)\). Under G1,
\(\pi^C_t=\rho\beta^\top(I-V)\mathbb{E}_t\pi_{t+1}+u^C_t\): the whole
CPI response is the cost-push term and its expected continuation.

\subsubsection{5.6 The IS curve (needed only when G1 is
relaxed)}\label{the-is-curve-needed-only-when-g1-is-relaxed}

Log-linearizing (2.3) with \(c_t=y_t\):
\(y_t=\mathbb{E}_ty_{t+1}-\frac1\gamma\big(i_t-\mathbb{E}_t\pi^C_{t+1}+\log\rho\big)\).
Subtracting the natural counterpart and defining the natural rate
\(r^n_t\equiv-\log\rho+\gamma\,\mathbb{E}_t\big(y^{nat}_{t+1}-y^{nat}_t\big)\),
\[\tilde y_t=\mathbb{E}_t\tilde y_{t+1}-\frac1\gamma\big(i_t-\mathbb{E}_t\pi^C_{t+1}-r^n_t\big),\qquad r^n_t=-\log\rho+\gamma(\eta-1)\frac{1+\phi}{\gamma+\phi}\lambda^\top\log A_t . \tag{5.21}\]
Note that \(r^n_t\) loads on \(\log A_t\) through \(\lambda=\lambda^*\):
a regime change moves the natural rate's sensitivity to every sectoral
shock, a third channel (beyond exposure and slope) through which the
network affects dynamics once the gap is not closed.

\subsection{6. (Main) Issues}\label{consistency-checks-issues-mapped-to-assumptions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Frequency separation: producers evaluate the technique choice problem at flex-price objects that they do not face in reality.} This assumption guarantees a closed form and allows most of the network-block theorems to hold as in KMNT paper. However, this omits a channel of technique choice: producers are pushed toward sticky-price
suppliers. Under M4 price stickiness is the same between foreign and domestic, so this issue does not affect reshoring/delocalization decisions.
    \item \textbf{Foreign producers buy labor from the labor union just as all the other domestic sectors.} This keeps the input-output matrix invertible and thus guarantees the existence of the Leontief inverse. Need a way to incorporate imperfect labor mobility. Can changing the model of wage rigidity (dropping union sector in $\Omega_i$) be a solution?
\end{itemize}








\clearpage
Each item states the issue, the assumption(s) responsible, and the
resolution or the flag to carry.

\textbf{1. The inflationary sign of a foreign supply shock depends on
wage rigidity, not on the network.} From (5.20), in the one-sector
economy (union \(0\), final good \(C\), \(\Omega_{C0}=1\)) one finds
\(v^C_C=-\hat\delta_C(1-\hat\delta_L)/(1-\hat\delta_C\hat\delta_L)\): a
negative productivity shock raises CPI cost-push only if
\(\hat\delta_L<1\). With flexible wages (\(\hat\delta_L=1\)) the
residual is zero --- the divine coincidence --- and in multi-sector
economies with flexible wages an upstream negative shock is
\emph{deflationary} (R23 Example 2). \emph{Source:} N1 with
\(\delta_L<1\). \emph{Status:} intended; N1 must not be relaxed to
flexible wages. \textcolor{blue}{We know this from macro 101. In the single sector economy without wage rigidity we have divine coincidence}

\textbf{2. The technique choice of a monopolistic producer has a
second-order condition that KMNT's price-taker does not.} Equation
(4.9): \(H_i+(\theta-1)\Sigma_p\prec0\) on the simplex tangent space. A
CES monopolist's profit is convex in own cost (4.1), so it values cost
variance with weight \(\theta-1\); if the TFP shifter is not curved
enough the producer prefers a corner technique to gamble on cost.
\emph{Source:} M1 (CES, monopolistic competition, needed for Calvo)
combined with T2 (finite curvature \(H_i\)). \emph{Resolution:} impose
\(\bar\kappa\) large (A5 of the design document), which makes \(-H_i\)
dominate; verify (4.9) numerically at the calibrated \(\Sigma_p\). The
first-order condition (4.7) is unaffected, so all KMNT comparative
statics still apply. \textcolor{blue}{Mention to MW because it is a difference compared to the KMNT paper. The assumption of large $\bar \kappa$ makes sense and is used in KMNT, as well.}

\textbf{3. Risk aversion $\gamma$ in the network block requires \(\gamma>1\),
which makes hours countercyclical to productivity.} \(\Gamma>0\) iff
\(\gamma>1\) (P3); then (3.6) gives
\(l^{nat}=\frac{1-\gamma}{\gamma+\phi}z<0\) for \(z>0\). \emph{Source:}
P1, P3, E1 (no capital to absorb the income effect). \emph{Status:} a
known property of the textbook NK model with \(\gamma>1\); acceptable
for the questions asked, but note that the same parameter drives both
the flight-to-safety motive and the hours response. \textcolor{blue}{Also this assumption of $\gamma > 1$ makes sense and is used in KMNT, as well. See footnote 10 in paper w/ supplement.}

\textbf{4. Frequency separation evaluates the technique at flex-price
objects that the producer does not actually face.} Under G1,
\(c_t=c^{nat}_t\) to first order, so \(\tilde\lambda\) in (4.4) is
correct to first order; but sectoral prices and quantities deviate from
their natural values at first order, and a Calvo producer stuck at an
old price bears input-cost variance directly in its markup --- a hedging
motive that (4.7) omits. \emph{Source:} I2. \emph{Status:} the
approximation error is small when shocks are persistent or the sourcing
horizon is long (variance-ratio calculation in the design discussion);
the omitted motive would push producers \emph{toward} sticky-price
suppliers, which under M4 is neutral between foreign and domestic. Flag
as extension. \textcolor{red}{MAIN DISCUSSION POINT WITH MW.}

\textbf{5. Techniques use unconditional moments although the current
\(\varepsilon_0\) may be known.} (4.3) replaced
\(\sum_t\rho^t\mathbb{E}[\cdot]\) by a single unconditional expectation.
With AR(1) shocks (E3) the conditional distribution at the start of the
regime differs from the unconditional one. \emph{Source:} I1.
\emph{Resolution:} interpret the choice as made behind the veil, or as
the average over the regime's stationary distribution; either is exact
if the regime is long relative to \(1/(1-\eta)\).

\textbf{6. A regime change moves the linearization point.} N2 linearizes
around the steady state of the regime, which has network
\(\Omega^*(\mu,\Sigma)\) and TFP levels \(\mu+a(\Omega^*)\). After a
fragmentation shock, \(\Omega^*\) changes discretely, and inherited
prices \(p_{t-1}\) were set under the old steady state. Computing the
transition with the new \((\Omega^*,V)\) and
\(\chi_t=p_{t-1}+L\log A_t\) treats the difference between old and new
natural prices as a one-off shock, which is valid to first order only if
\(\Delta\Omega\) is small. \emph{Source:} N2, E4, T3. \emph{Resolution:}
keep \(\bar\kappa\) large (then \(\Delta\Omega=O(\bar\kappa^{-1})\),
KMNT Lemma 9); otherwise compute the transition numerically as a change
of steady state.

\textbf{7. Ex-post shares are constant, so there is no price-induced
reallocation.} Under T1 a permanent rise in a foreign price does not
change \(\Omega_{if}\) ex post; all network movement is ex ante through
\(R\). \emph{Source:} T1 (needed for exact log-linearity of prices and
for (4.5) to be Gaussian). \emph{Status:} a substantive restriction,
stated openly; the robustness check is a CES nest between \(F\) and
\(D\) in the ex-post technology, which leaves (5.12) unchanged to first
order (cost shares are sufficient statistics by Shephard's lemma) but
adds a direct price elasticity to \(\Omega\). \textcolor{blue}{Yes. A realized rise in a foreign price within a regime, however persistent, leaves \(\Omega\) unchanged; \(\Omega\) moves only when beliefs \((\mu, \Sigma)\) change. But my model is all about beliefs. I don't really care about within-regime network adjustment.}

\textbf{8. The log-normal mean effect.} A rise in \(\Sigma_{ii}\) raises
\(\mathbb{E}[e^{\varepsilon_i}]\) by \(\tfrac12\Sigma_{ii}\); the
objective (4.11) is in logs and does not see this, but level comparisons
of GDP across regimes would. \emph{Source:} E3. \emph{Status:} KMNT App.
H; ignore in the baseline and say so.

\textbf{9. Correction to an earlier claim: the fully flexible limit is
\(0/0\), not zero.} As \(\Delta\to I\), \(\delta^\beta\to\lambda^\top\)
and \(\delta^\beta\alpha\to1\), so both numerator and denominator of
(5.20) vanish. The limit depends on the direction of approach: with
\(\hat\delta_L=1\) and any \(\hat\delta_C\) in the one-sector economy,
\(v^C_C=0\); with uniform \(\hat\delta_i=1-\epsilon\), \(\epsilon\to0\),
\(v^C_C\to-\tfrac12\) --- a TFP shock is split evenly between price and
wage adjustment however small the rigidity, because what matters is
\emph{relative} stickiness. The statement in
\texttt{model\_foundations.md} §7.2 that ``\(v^C\equiv0\) under fully
flexible prices'' is wrong and should read: the CPI residual vanishes
when wages are flexible and there is a single final sector (divine
coincidence); in general it depends on the ratio of wage to price
rigidity even in the flexible limit. \emph{Source:} N1. \emph{Status:}
corrected below in the design document.

\textbf{10. Sectors with \(\delta_i=1\) make \(\kappa_i\) infinite.}
(5.4) degenerates to \(mc_{it}=p_{it}\), and \(K\) in (5.5) is
unbounded; R23's invertibility caveat for \(I-V\) is the same fact.
\emph{Source:} N1. \emph{Status:} non-binding under M4
(\(\delta_F=\delta_D<1\)); in the commodity variant, substitute the
static condition for that sector rather than using \(\kappa_i\).

\textbf{11. The union's Phillips curve has the standard slope only
because of pooling.} Without P4, each labor type's marginal rate of
substitution depends on its own hours, which depend on its relative
wage, and the wage Phillips curve slope is \(\kappa_0/(1+\theta\phi)\)
(Erceg--Henderson--Levin). \emph{Source:} P4. \emph{Status:} intended
simplification; the union is ``just a sector'' only under P4. \textcolor{blue}{GTK, but the assumption is standard in the literature(?)}

\textbf{12. Subsidy financing and profits under sticky prices.} In the
flex economy the subsidy equals aggregate profit and \(P^CC=WL\) exactly
(3.4). Under sticky prices profits differ from the subsidy at first
order, but the difference is a lump-sum transfer to a Ricardian
household and does not affect first-order allocations. \emph{Source:}
M2, E1 (no distortionary taxes). \emph{Status:} fine.

\textbf{13. \(y_t=c_t\) and \(\tilde y_t=\tilde l_t\) are first-order
statements under sticky prices.} Price dispersion within sectors and
relative-price misallocation across sectors are second order at an
efficient steady state. \emph{Source:} N2, M2. \emph{Status:} fine;
would fail if the steady state were distorted (no subsidy), in which
case first-order terms from misallocation appear and R23's welfare block
would be needed.

\textbf{14. Closing the gap requires the central bank to know
\(\lambda^*\).} After a regime change
\(y^{nat}_t=\frac{1+\phi}{\gamma+\phi}\lambda^{*\top}\log A_t\) loads on
shocks through the new Domar weights; a bank using the old \(\lambda\)
would misjudge the natural level. \emph{Source:} I3, G1. \emph{Status:}
assumed; an informational-friction extension is possible but off the
main line.

\textbf{15. Foreign sectors buy domestic labor services.} Under M4 a
foreign supplier's marginal cost (2.5) contains the domestic wage
\(p_{0t}\), so domestic wage shocks transmit into ``foreign'' prices and
foreign productivity shocks feed back into domestic labor demand exactly
as domestic ones do. This is unrealistic for imports. \emph{Source:} M4
(the price of keeping \(L\alpha=\mathbf{1}\) and R23's lemmas intact).
\emph{Status:} the open-economy extension replaces the foreign sector's
labor input with an exogenous factor; until then, ``foreign'' means
``risky and productive'', not ``abroad''.

\textbf{16. Efficiency of foreign sourcing is parameterized twice.} A
foreign input can be attractive through a high \(\mu_f\) (cheap) or a
high ideal share \(\Omega^\circ_{if}\) (technologically preferred). Both
enter \(R\) and \(\Omega^*\) but only \(\mu_f\) interacts with risk
through \(\mathcal{E}=\mu-\Gamma\Sigma\lambda\). \emph{Source:} T2, E3.
\emph{Status:} choose one as the ``efficiency side'' of the trade-off
and hold the other fixed, to keep comparative statics interpretable.

\textbf{17. \(\hat\delta_i\) is not the Calvo reset probability.} By
(5.13) a quarterly reset probability \(\delta_i=0.25\) implies
\(\hat\delta_i\approx0.08\), and \(\hat\delta_i=0.25\) corresponds to
\(\delta_i\approx0.44\). The illustrative calibrations used earlier in
this project were stated in \(\hat\delta\) units. \emph{Source:} N1.
\emph{Status:} calibrate \(\delta_i\) from price-duration data and
convert with (5.13); do not set \(\hat\delta\) directly.

\textbf{18. Atomistic technique choice is consistent with one technique
per sector.} In (4.3) the producer takes \(\lambda_i,P_i,Y_i\) as given;
its own \(\Omega_{if}\) does not move them because it has measure zero
(M1). Symmetry then delivers T3. No issue, but T3 is a consequence of M1
plus the uniqueness of the maximizer (which is where (4.9) matters), not
an independent assumption.
